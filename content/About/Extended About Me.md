If you're on this page, I presume it is because you want to learn more about me as a person/idea/philosophy/enigma/social experiment/etc. aside from my academic and professional pursuits. This page will be more-or-less a long ramble about the stories and views that have shaped who I am and my approach to engineering.

I was raised and educated in Richmond, BC, a municipality within the Metro Vancouver area that is best known for fishing, agriculture, Chinese food, and old people. I did not have a particularly remarkable childhood. My mother and step-father both attended UBC, where they studied biochemistry and civil engineering respectively. My older step-brother graduated from UBC recently with a degree in French and computer science. My older step-sister, arguably the smartest sibling, is currently studying engineering physics at the same institution. As a result, the clear path for me was to attend UofT out of spite of the family tradition (this is a joke, mostly).

I spent most of my life online, as is the case for the majority of young people in my generation. In the real world, I was quite lonely, isolated, and self-loathing due to bullying and ostracization, so staring at computers for hours at a time was my escape. Through the internet, I developed intense fixations on various topics, including the Chernobyl nuclear disaster. I watched (and rewatched) the HBO mini-series _Chernobyl_ (2019), I wrote, produced, and narrated a podcast about Chernobyl, I completed various school projects on nuclear energy, and I ate up any books about nuclear engineering and its history that I could get my hands on. This "autism-fueled obsession" (as my friends like to put it) ended up being the catalyst for me to seriously pursue engineering.

I have been asked why I have (currently) chosen to pursue a career in academia rather than industry.
