**TW:** Discussions of discrimination and sexual violence

Both the city of Toronto and the University of Toronto are incredibly diverse and multicultural environments. That being said, the fields of engineering, science, and mathematics have only recently begun breaking down the walls for non-male, non-white, and otherwise minority demographics. It would be dishonest for me to say that their reputations for being inaccessible and exclusive aren't well-deserved. 

Sexism, racism, homophobia, xenophobia, ableism, and other forms of oppression are not relics of the past--they are still very much alive in every aspect of this field. To deny this fact is not only inaccurate, but directly complicit in the perpetuation of these discriminations. Thus, I urge everyone to do the work of educating oneself on the experiences of marginalized people and advocate for a better environment for everyone.

It is not uncommon for women and minorities to receive comments that they only received their positions/achievements/etc. due to their marginalized status (cough, "*DEI*", cough). I have certainly heard several students make comments along these lines during my time here. Regardless if these claims are true or not, it is a malicious, hurtful sentiment that should not be tolerated. I can assure you if you've made it to UofT Engineering, *you belong here*. Skule (TM) would not be the same without its diverse community.

I have thankfully never been on the receiving end of sexual violence here at UofT, though it does happen and it's not always well addressed by the institution. Sadly, there is a long history of professors, TAs, and other students (many who are still currently active in the UofT community) taking advantage of their positions of authority, power dynamics, or institutional protection. The sphere of higher-education relies heavily on discretion of superiors for grades and career advancement, and peers for social acceptance and belonging--creating an environment that allows these abuses of power to happen. Because the university's official reporting channels are often very bureaucratic and inefficient, [survivors frequently face a lot of barriers](https://thepearsproject.wordpress.com/projects-publications/surviving-the-centre-video/). 

If you or someone you know experiences harassment, discrimination, or violence, you do not need to navigate it alone. Student-run groups, such as PEARS (listed below) offer survivor-centric, confidential support. I believe you.
