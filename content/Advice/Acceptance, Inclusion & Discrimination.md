Both the city of Toronto and the University of Toronto are incredibly diverse and multicutural environments. That being said, the fields of engineering, science, and mathematics have only recently begun breaking down the walls for non-male, non-white, and otherwise minority demographics. It would be dishonest for me to say that their reputations for being inaccessible and exclusive aren't well-deserved. 

I hope that I can provide some guidance on navigating these spaces in a genuine, honest way without fear-mongering. Please keep in mind that I am only speaking from the perspective of a queer, East/Southeast Asian, and disabled person--I cannot speak on the behalf of other groups of people.

- For women and other gender minorities--yes, engineering still is very much a "boy's club". While the undergraduate student population is roughly 40% female, most of your professors and other higher-ups will be cisgender men. Sometimes you will be the only, or one of few, women in the room.
	- I have personally never experienced direct, explicit sexist comments, though I have faced subtle microaggressions. I have witnessed men ignore, speak over, paternalize, and "one-up" women; even if those women are just as if not more competent than them.
	- 
	- Obligatory *"not all men"* disclaimer: I have several male friends, mentors, and professors who are supportive allies to women. These men do exist!
