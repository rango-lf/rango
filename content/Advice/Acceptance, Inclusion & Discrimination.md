>[!info] WORK IN PROGRESS

***
Both the city of Toronto and the University of Toronto are incredibly diverse and multicultural environments. That being said, the fields of engineering, science, and mathematics have only recently begun breaking down the walls for non-male, non-white, and otherwise minority demographics. It would be dishonest for me to say that their reputations for being inaccessible and exclusive aren't well-deserved. 

Sexism, racism, homophobia, xenophobia, ableism, and other forms of oppression are not relics of the past--they are still very much alive in every aspect of this field. To deny this fact is not only inaccurate, but directly complicit in the perpetuation of these discriminations. Thus, I urge everyone to do the work of educating oneself on the experiences of marginalized people and advocate for a better environment for everyone.

It is not uncommon for women and minorities to receive comments that they only received their positions/achievements/etc. due to their marginalized status (cough, "*DEI*", cough). I have certainly heard several students make comments along these lines during my time here.
