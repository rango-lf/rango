## Fall Semester
#### APS100 — Orientation to Engineering

| Difficulty | Workload |
| ---------- | :------: |
| 2/10       |   3/10   |
Tips
- Attend most lectures/tutorials and submit all assignments. That's really all you need to pass
- Don't over-invest here; save energy for your other 5 courses
- **Get to know your TA**; they're upper-year students with real insight on surviving first year
- Skip past tests for exam prep, as the content changes yearly. Use lecture slides and the provided study guide instead

#### APS110 / APS164 — Engineering Chemistry & Materials Science

Textbook problems · quizzes · term test · final exam

| Difficulty | Workload |
| ---------- | :------: |
| 4/10       |   6/10   |
Tips

- **Do the textbook problems**. They're a free ~5% and build understanding, but focus on assignments and past exams for exam prep
- Write down key equations and note _when_ and _how_ to use each one
- Start studying early. There's lots of content, easy to fall behind if you cram
- Materials science is newer/harder than the chemistry half; budget extra time for it

#### APS111 — Engineering Strategies & Practice I

60% individual · 40% team · semester-long design project

| Difficulty | Workload |
| ---------- | :------: |
| 7/10       |   9/10   |

Tips
- Collect all **PD points** (0.1% each); easy free marks from events like Myfab Safety Training
- Follow the assignment reference handbook and self-assessment lists closely for the PR and CDS
- Set internal deadlines **2 days early**. Make sure you catch mistakes and who isn't contributing enough
- Figure out what your TA/CI prioritizes early; marking varies a lot between sections
- Bad group? Document everything (edit history, chat logs, meeting minutes) and contact your TA/CI early before issues escalate

#### CIV100 / APS160 — Mechanics

Weekly assignments · quizzes · midterm · final exam

| Difficulty | Workload |
| ---------- | :------: |
| 7/10       |   7/10   |

Tips
- Consider **APS160** (online version) if you have a long commute or want more scheduling flexibility
- Weekly assignments take hours but are the best way to actually understand the material. Try not to cheat or skip them.
- **Past exams are your best friend**. Questions are often recycled or slightly modified year to year
- Don't panic after a bad midterm; final grades are almost always curved
- If your professor isn't working for you, use YouTube or online tutorials. Good alternatives (e.g. Jeff Hansen) exist.
- APS160 note: Mastering Engineering is painful and you only get two attempts per question. You can request regrades from TAs with your work shown

#### MAT186 / APS162 — Calculus I

Pre-class essentials · 2 term tests · final exam

| Difficulty | Workload |
| ---------- | :------: |
| 8/10       |   6/10   

Tips
- **Don't underestimate this course**. It is significantly harder, faster-paced, and deeper than high school calculus
- Do the Pre-Class Essentials properly; they prepare you for lectures (lowest 3 are dropped, so don't waste that buffer)
- Attend lectures and work through in-class problems 
- Master the basics before leaning on past exams
- Go to office hours, GEARS, etc. early!

#### MAT188 — Linear Algebra

Standards-based grading · ~30% from weekly work · 35% final

| Difficulty | Workload |
| ---------- | :------: |
| 6/10       |   7/10   |

Tips
- **Never skip the free marks**. Tutorial quizzes, assignments, and PCEs are ~30% of your grade and keep you on pace
- Concepts build on each other fast; falling behind makes everything exponentially harder
- Past midterms may not reflect your professor's style. Try to focus on PCEs and learning standards for exam prep
- If you can, study ahead over the summer. This is the hardest course to recover in after you fall behind
- If confused, go to Prof. Shai Cohen's office hours!
