>[!info] This guide was specifically written with University of Toronto Engineering students in mind. This is also based on my personal experience, and may not be universally applicable. 

***
## So You Want to Get Into Research
The University of Toronto is Canada's top research university, which means opportunities to get involved are quite abundant. However, bagging one of these opportunities means you have to know where to look and how to ask.

I got a summer research position in my first year in the Department of Mechanical and Industrial Engineering. My project focused on maternal and infant health outcomes, which wasn't something I ever expected to work on, but more on that later. 

Why bother? Getting into research as an undergrad has the following benefits that I think many people take for granted:
- **Explore your interests** and develop a much deeper understanding of topics you're curious about.
- **Build skills** such as collaboration, communication, critical thinking, and technical skills (e.g., using certain software, wet lab techniques, literature review) that you may not learn in a classroom. 
- **Figure out your passions:** research is a great way to discover (or rule out) potential career paths. Some of my friends who were previously interested in pursuing a career in academia found out through undergrad research that it actually didn't suit them, and vice versa.
- **Network with faculty** who can become valuable references, mentors, and industry connections later down the line. Assuming you do good work, you may receive a return offer from your supervisor for your research thesis or grad school.
- **Strengthen your CV and resume** for grad school especially, and job applications.
- **Earn money:** many research positions are paid.
- **Check off your PEY/practical experience requirement:** research work counts towards UofT Engineering's [practical experience requirement](https://engineering.calendar.utoronto.ca/curriculum#practical).
## Getting into Research (Tips & Tricks)
Make sure you **start early**. If you are interested in a summer research position, ideally begin the process around fall reading week. A good target is to start contacting professors right after winter break when everyone is coming back, or a few weeks before fall finals if you want to get ahead of the curve.

**Know your interests but stay open minded.** It helps to go in with some idea of what fields or topics appeal to you. That being said, don't let a lack of certainty stop you. I never would have guessed I'd end up doing research in healthcare. I didn't even know there was an intersection between engineering (especially industrial/systems engineering) and healthcare until I stumbled into it. 

**Do your research on the research**. Browse your department's research and faculty pages to see what projects are active. You can also search professors by faculty and research interests through the UofT [Discover Research directory](https://discover.research.utoronto.ca/). Look for work that actually interests you, as professors want students who are enthusiastic about their field of study.

**Stay organized.** This is not universally necessary, but I found it helpful to keep track of professor's contact info, research areas, and any notes in a spreadsheet or Google Doc. It makes follow-up much easier and helps you personalize each email.

Some professors will request to see your **transcript and/or CV/resume**, as they may have a GPA cut-off for undergrad researchers or they just want to make sure you aren't failing. It is best to have those two documents prepared before contacting them. Your unofficial transcript can be found on your [ACORN account](https://www.registrar.utoronto.ca/records-academics/transcripts/). 

Also, **some professors may request an interview with you**. The nature of this interview varies greatly depending on the person, but regardless I'd suggest preparing some notes that include your research interests, what you hope to get out of research, skillsets you can bring to their lab, etc. My supervisor was very chill, it was basically like a conversation where she asked me about my first year experience and why I chose to attend UofT.
## Cold Email Example
Cold-emailing is probably the best way to reach out to a professor for research opportunities. Keep it concise, enthusiastic, and specific. Below is a template to work from, but please ensure you tailor it for each professor/lab:

**Subject: Inquiry About Summer Research Opportunities in [Professor’s Area of Expertise]**

> Dear Prof/Dr. [name],
> 
> I hope this email finds you well.
> 
> My name is [name] and I am a [major] student at UofT. I am deeply interested in [specific area of research], and I am eager to gain hands-on experience in research that aligns with your work on [mention a specific project, paper, or area from their research].
> 
> I have been developing a strong foundation in [relevant coursework or skills, e.g., 3D printing, CAD modeling, MATLAB programming], and I am highly motivated to contribute to your team’s ongoing projects. I am particularly drawn to your research because [briefly explain why—impact, innovation, or personal interest].
> 
> Would you be open to discussing potential opportunities for me to assist in your lab during [term & year]? I am flexible regarding the scope of work and willing to learn quickly to meet your expectations. I can provide my resume and transcript upon request.
> 
> Thank you for considering my inquiry.
> 
> Best regards,
> [name & pronouns]
> [email]
> [department of study]

Some other things to keep in mind:
- Always use your UofT email(!!!)
- Professors are busy, so don't take a non-response personally.
- Follow up once after about a week if you haven't heard back.
## Funding
Note that the specific dollar amounts and deadlines above are based on the 2026 competition cycle and may change year to year. Always check the official pages for the most current information. Here are the main funding avenues to be aware of:

**[First-Year Summer Research Fellowship](https://undergrad.engineering.utoronto.ca/first-year-office-2/first-year-updates-deadlines/first-year-summer-research-fellowships/)**: If you're a first-year student in a Core 8, TrackOne, or EngSci program, this one's specifically for you. The Faculty offers up to five of these fellowships each year, valued at $7,000 each, to support first-year students in gaining summer research experience. You're responsible for finding your own supervisor, and recipients are expected to present at UnERD in August. Worth knowing about early — the deadline tends to fall in late February. [utoronto](https://undergrad.engineering.utoronto.ca/first-year-office-2/first-year-updates-deadlines/first-year-summer-research-fellowships/)

**[NSERC USRA](https://www.registrar.utoronto.ca/financial-aid-awards/undergraduate-research-awards/) & [UTEA](https://www.registrar.utoronto.ca/financial-aid-awards/undergraduate-research-awards/)**: These are the two main undergraduate research award streams at UofT, and they run on a harmonized application process. Each award is valued at $6,000, with a minimum $1,500 top-up from the supervisor or division, for a total of $7,500 paid as a stipend. One key difference is that the USRA is open to Canadian citizens and permanent residents only, while the UTEA also accepts international students with a valid visa. The professor must apply on your behalf, so flag this early if you're interested. For Engineering students, the divisional deadline is typically in late February. Please check the [FASE undergraduate research page](https://undergrad.engineering.utoronto.ca/experiential-learning/research-opportunities/undergraduate-summer-research-awards/) for the current deadline.

**[Dean's Pivot Fellowship](https://undergrad.engineering.utoronto.ca/experiential-learning/research-opportunities/dean-summer-research-pivot-fellowship/)**: This is designed for collaborative projects co-supervised by two UofT faculty members, with at least one from FASE. The award is valued at $6,000 from the Faculty, with supervisors required to contribute an additional $2,000, for a total of $8,000. Up to 16 fellowships are awarded per year, and they're open to all Engineering undergrads; domestic and international.  Deadline is also typically in late February. 

**UofT Work-Study Program**: If you are taking at least one course during the summer, Work-Study can fund part of your position. Some professors specifically hire through this stream, so it's worth mentioning if you're eligible.

**Professor's Own Funding**: Some professors may fund undergrad researchers directly from their grants, without going through any of the formal award programs. If a professor is interested in having you, don't hesitate to ask whether they have funding available or which avenue they'd prefer to use.

Good luck on your research endeavours. The worst anyone can say is no, but most professors do appreciate hearing from motivated undergrads.
